📺 The Smart Shopper's Guide to TV Energy Costs in Australia

Buying a new TV? The upfront price is important, but for cost‑conscious families and students, the real cost over time can add up on your electricity bill. This data story guides you through the Australian TV market, showing how energy consumption varies dramatically based on your choice.

1. The Australian TV Market: Many Choices, One Clear Leader

The Australian market offers a wide range of TV screen technologies. As our analysis shows, LCD (LED) technology is the most dominant, representing the vast majority of available models.

You'll have the most options when choosing an LED TV. But energy efficiency varies, so it's important to look beyond just the technology type.

2. Screen Sizes: Something for Everyone

Manufacturers offer a diverse range of screen sizes, from compact 32-inch models to massive 100-inch home cinema displays. The distribution shows a preference for popular sizes like 50, 55, and 65 inches.

The wide variety means there's a TV for every room, from a bedroom to a living area.

3. Brand Power: Who Offers the Most Choice?

Samsung, LG, and Diviox lead the market in terms of product variety, giving consumers plenty of options to choose from.

More models don't always mean more energy efficient. It pays to compare.

4. Technology & Power: The Standout Energy Miser

Standard LCD technology has the lowest average power consumption, using significantly less energy than both LED-backlit LCDs (LCD LED) and OLED displays.

If minimising your electricity bill is your top priority, a standard LCD TV is your most energy‑efficient choice.

5. The Bigger They Are, The More They Consume

Larger screen sizes directly lead to higher power consumption. The scatter plot below clearly shows a positive correlation. Every extra inch adds to the energy bill.

Choosing a smaller screen is one of the most effective ways to keep your electricity costs down.

6. Star Ratings: Not as Straightforward as You Think

Our analysis shows a weak and inconsistent relationship between star rating and screen size. A large TV can still have a good star rating, and a small TV might have a poor one. Don't rely on stars alone.

Always compare the energy consumption number (in kWh per year) on the label, not just the stars, to get a true picture of running costs.

💡 Key Takeaways for the Smart Shopper

  • Prioritise size: The biggest factor in power consumption is screen size. Choose the smallest size you can comfortably live with.
  • Consider technology: Standard LCD TVs are typically the most energy‑efficient, though you may compromise on picture quality compared to LED or OLED.
  • Check the label: When comparing similar‑sized TVs, look at the yearly energy consumption (kWh/year) on the Energy Rating Label, not just the star rating.
  • Compare brands: Don't assume all brands are equal. Our data shows significant variation, so compare models side‑by‑side.
  • Calculate your cost: A TV that uses 213 kWh/year will cost about $60/year to run (at $0.28/kWh). A 520 kWh/year TV costs ~$146/year. That difference adds up over a few years!

By making an informed choice, you can enjoy your new TV without the shock of a high electricity bill. Happy (energy‑efficient) viewing!
