📺 The Smart Shopper's Guide to TV Energy Costs in Australia

Buying a new TV? The upfront price is important, but for cost‑conscious families and students, the real cost over time can add up on your electricity bill. This data story guides you through the Australian TV market, showing how energy consumption varies dramatically based on your choice.

1. The Australian TV Market: Many Choices, One Clear Leader

You'll have the most options when choosing an LED TV. But energy efficiency varies, so look beyond the technology type.

2. Screen Sizes: Something for Everyone

The wide variety means there's a TV for every room, from bedroom to living area.

3. Brand Power: Who Offers the Most Choice?

More models don't always mean more energy efficient. It pays to compare.

4. Technology & Power: The Standout Energy Miser

Standard LCD TV is your most energy‑efficient choice.

5. The Bigger They Are, The More They Consume

Choosing a smaller screen is one of the most effective ways to keep electricity costs down.

6. Star Ratings: Not as Straightforward as You Think

Always compare the energy consumption number (in kWh per year) on the label, not just the stars.

💡 Key Takeaways for the Smart Shopper

  • Prioritise size – biggest driver of power use.
  • Consider technology – standard LCD is most efficient.
  • Check the label – look at kWh/year, not just stars.
  • Compare brands – efficiency varies even at same size.
  • Calculate yearly cost – 213 kWh/year ~$60, 520 kWh/year ~$146 (at $0.28/kWh).

By making an informed choice, you can enjoy your new TV without the shock of a high electricity bill.

📋 Data Storyboard – Planning & Narrative Flow

Audience: Cost‑conscious Australian families & students | Goal: Guide smart, energy‑efficient TV buying | Narrative arc: Market overview → Size variety → Brand choice → Power efficiency → Size vs. power → Star rating reality → Actionable takeaways

STEP 1

Market landscape: Which screen technologies are most common?

❓ Audience question: "What are my options?"
📊 Bar chart | X: Screen technology | Y: Count of models

Insight: LCD (LED) dominates (>900 models). Shoppers have widest choice here.

Why bar chart: Best for comparing discrete categories – heights are easy to compare.

STEP 2

Screen size availability

❓ "What sizes can I buy?"
📊 Bar chart | X: Size (inches) | Y: Number of models

Insight: Wide range (32"–100"+), peaks at 50", 55", 65". Something for every room.

Why bar chart: Shows frequency distribution clearly – easy to spot popular sizes.

STEP 3

Brand variety: Who offers the most models?

❓ "Which brands should I consider?"
📊 Bar chart | X: Brand | Y: Distinct model count

Insight: Samsung, LG, Diviox lead. But more models ≠ more efficiency.

Why bar chart: Easy to see top brands at a glance.

STEP 4

Power consumption by technology

❓ "Which technology saves me the most on electricity?"
📊 Bar chart | X: Technology | Y: Avg power (Watts)

Insight: Standard LCD = 87W (most efficient). LED‑LCD 119W, OLED 129W.

Why bar chart: Direct comparison of average values – shortest bar is best.

STEP 5

Relationship: Screen size vs. Power consumption

❓ "Does a bigger screen cost more to run?"
📈 Scatter plot | X: Screen size | Y: Power (Watts)

Insight: Strong positive correlation – every inch adds to your bill.

Why scatter plot: Best for showing relationship between two continuous variables.

STEP 6

Star rating vs. Screen size – does it help?

❓ "Can I trust the star rating?"
📈 Scatter plot | X: Screen size | Y: Star rating

Insight: Weak/no relationship – stars alone are misleading. Always check kWh/year.

Why scatter plot: Shows lack of correlation – points are scattered randomly.

🎯 Final Call‑to‑Action (Conclusion)

  • Prioritise size – biggest driver of power use.
  • Consider LCD – most energy‑efficient technology.
  • Check the label – look at kWh/year, not just stars.
  • Compare brands – efficiency varies even at same size.
  • Calculate yearly cost – 213 kWh/year = ~$60, 520 kWh/year = ~$146.

Audience message: Save money without sacrificing enjoyment – choose wisely.
